## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.
Definition:

This problem statement explains how agricultural products pass through many middlemen, such as agents, wholesalers and retailers, before reaching consumers. Each intermediary adds extra charges or profit margins, which reduces the amount of money farmers receive and increases the final price paid by consumers.

As a result, farmers earn less despite their hard work, while consumers pay more for agricultural products.

In simple words:Farmers receive less money for their crops, but consumers pay higher prices because too many middlemen are involved between them.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

It is a department of the Government of India that works to protect consumer rights, ensure the availability of essential food items, maintain fair prices and manage the distribution of food grains to people.

In simple words: This ministry works to ensure that consumers get essential products at reasonable prices, receive proper food supplies and are protected from unfair trade practices.

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
We propose an AI-powered digital marketplace called Farm2Door that directly connects farmers, FPOs, consumers and bulk buyers without unnecessary intermediaries.
- Farmers can sell agricultural products directly to customers.
- Buyers can compare prices and purchase fresh products.
- AI predicts product demand and helps farmers decide how much to produce and supply.
- A logistics system connects farmers with delivery partners.
- Transparent pricing ensures fair earnings for farmers and affordable prices for consumers.

## Technical Approach
Technologies Used:
- Frontend: HTML, CSS, JavaScript.
- Backend: Python and Django.
- Database: MySQL.
- AI/ML: Demand forecasting and price prediction.
- Google Maps API: Delivery route optimization.
- Payment Gateway: Secure online transactions.
Implementation Process:
1. Register farmers, FPOs and buyers.
2. Allow farmers to upload products and prices.
3. Display products in a digital marketplace.
4. Enable consumers and bulk buyers to place orders.
5. Use AI to forecast demand.
6. Optimize delivery routes using location data.
7. Complete delivery and direct farmer payments.
## Feasibility and Viability
Feasibility:
- Can be developed using affordable existing technologies.
- Accessible through smartphones and computers.
- Can support small-scale and large-scale farmers.
- Can be expanded to different villages and cities.
Potential Challenges and Risks:
- Poor internet connectivity in rural areas.
- Lack of digital knowledge among farmers.
- Transportation and storage difficulties.
- Agricultural price fluctuations.
- Product damage during delivery.
Strategies to Overcome Challenges:
- Provide a simple multilingual interface.
- Offer farmer training and technical support.
- Partner with local transport services.
- Use AI-based price and demand analysis.
- Introduce proper packaging and storage facilities.
## Impact and Benefits
Farmers: Higher income through direct selling.

It means farmers can sell their agricultural products directly to consumers instead of depending on middlemen. This helps farmers receive a larger share of the selling price and increase their income.

Consumers: Fresh agricultural products at lower prices.

It means consumers can buy fresh fruits, vegetables and other agricultural products directly from farmers at affordable prices because unnecessary middlemen and additional costs are reduced.


FPOs: Better market access and bulk selling opportunities.


FPOs (Farmer Producer Organizations) help groups of farmers sell their agricultural products together in large quantities. This gives them access to more buyers, better markets and opportunities to earn higher profits.

Economy: Reduced unnecessary intermediary costs.


It means reducing the extra charges paid to middlemen during the buying and selling of agricultural products. This helps farmers earn more and consumers save money.


Environment: Less food wastage and optimized transportation.


It means reducing the waste of fruits, vegetables and other food products by delivering them quickly and using shorter, efficient delivery routes. This also saves fuel and reduces pollution.


Society: Improved rural development and digital agriculture.


It means using modern technology to help farmers sell their products easily, improve their farming methods, create better employment opportunities and improve the living standards of people in villages.
